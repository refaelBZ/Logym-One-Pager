To customize a workout plan, keep its structure and main lifts, then change one thing at a time: swap an exercise for a similar movement, adjust the rep range, or fit the days to your schedule. Small, deliberate changes keep the plan working and keep your log comparable.

## What to keep the same

- **The weekly structure.** If it is an upper/lower plan, keep upper and lower days.
- **The main lifts.** These carry most of your progress, so keep them for as long as they improve.
- **A progression rule.** Whatever you change, keep a clear way to add reps or weight.

## How to customize a workout plan in five steps

1. **Fit the days.** Move sessions to days you can train. Keep at least a day between hard sessions for the same muscles when possible.
2. **Swap like for like.** Replace an exercise with one that trains the same movement. A barbell row can become a dumbbell row; a back squat can become a goblet squat or leg press.
3. **Adjust rep ranges.** If a range never feels right, shift it slightly, such as 6 to 10 instead of 5 to 8.
4. **Set sets you can finish.** If you cannot complete the planned sets in your time, trim accessory sets first.
5. **Change one thing per week.** That way your log shows what made the difference.

## Good swaps and bad swaps

| Original | Good swap | Poor swap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Bench press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_press) | Dumbbell bench press | Biceps curl |
| [Squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squat_(exercise)) | Leg press or goblet squat | Calf raise |
| [Bent-over row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bent-over_row) | Seated cable row | Shrug |
| Pull-up | Lat pulldown | Push-up |

Good swaps train the same pattern. Poor swaps leave a gap in the plan.

## An example

An illustrative case: a plan calls for barbell squats, but the rack is always busy at your gym time. You swap to a leg press, keep 3 sets of 8 to 12, and log it as a new exercise. Two weeks later your leg press numbers are climbing and the rest of the plan is unchanged. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.

## When customizing becomes starting over

If you change most exercises, days, and rep ranges at once, you have a new plan. That is fine, but treat it as one and give it time.
