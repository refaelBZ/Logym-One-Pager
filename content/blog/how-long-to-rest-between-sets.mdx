How long should you rest between sets? A practical rule for strength training is about 2 to 3 minutes for heavy compound lifts like squats, deadlifts, and presses, and about 1 to 2 minutes for lighter or single-joint exercises like curls and lateral raises. Rest long enough that you can do your next set with good form and close to the reps you planned.

## Rest times by type of exercise

| Exercise type | Examples | Practical rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy compound lifts | Squat, deadlift, bench press | 2 to 3 minutes, longer on very heavy sets |
| Moderate compound lifts | Rows, lunges, leg press | About 1.5 to 2 minutes |
| Single-joint exercises | Curls, triceps extensions, lateral raises | About 1 to 1.5 minutes |

These are starting points. If you regularly fall well short of your target reps on later sets, rest a bit longer.

## Why rest length matters

Short rest leaves less time to catch your breath before the next set, so your reps often drop. Longer rest usually lets you repeat a similar performance. Neither is wrong, but changing rest from session to session makes your numbers hard to compare. If you rested 90 seconds last week and 4 minutes today, extra reps may come from the rest, not from getting stronger.

## How to keep rest consistent

1. Pick a rest time for each exercise based on the table above.
2. Use a timer or the clock on the wall, so you are not guessing.
3. Log each set during your rest, which keeps you from drifting into long breaks. The guide on [how to log sets during your workout](/blog/how-to-log-sets-during-your-workout) covers this habit.
4. Keep the same rest times for a few weeks, so changes in your reps reflect real progress.

## When to change your rest

Rest a little longer if your form breaks down on later sets, or if your reps drop sharply from set to set. Shorter rest can be fine for lighter exercises at the end of a workout when time is tight. If you feel dizzy or unwell, stop and consult a qualified professional.

## An example

Illustrative bench press session with 2.5 minutes of rest:

| Set | Weight | Reps | Rest after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 60 kg | 8 | 2.5 min |
| 2 | 60 kg | 8 | 2.5 min |
| 3 | 60 kg | 7 | Done |

Keeping the same rest next week means an extra rep on set 3 shows real progress, which is the idea behind [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload). For other first-month basics, see the [beginner's guide to strength training](/blog/beginners-guide-to-strength-training).
