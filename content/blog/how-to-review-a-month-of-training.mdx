To review a month of training, put week 1 next to week 4 for each main lift and note whether weight, reps, or completed sessions moved. A weekly check picks next session's target. A monthly check answers a bigger question: is this plan still producing progress?

The five-minute habit in [how to review your training week](/blog/how-to-review-your-training-week) stays useful. Once a month, zoom out so one bad Wednesday does not set the story. The free [weekly training review](/tools/weekly-training-review) covers the weekly pass you can stack into a month.

## How to review a month of training

Set aside ten to fifteen minutes with the log open.

1. **List the main lifts only.** Squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press, and one or two rows or pulls are enough. Ignore a different finisher you tried once.
2. **Mark planned sessions versus sessions you completed.** A lift that "stalled" during a month you attended half the time is an attendance problem first.
3. **Compare the first week and the last week** on weight and reps for the same exercise. Write one line: up, flat, or down.
4. **Decide one action per lift.** Keep the load, add a small jump, hold for reps, or flag a stall. [How long to follow a workout program](/blog/how-long-to-follow-a-workout-program) is the backdrop: a month is a checkpoint, not an automatic reason to switch plans.

## A monthly snapshot

Illustrative best sets:

| Lift | Week 1 | Week 4 | Call |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 100 kg x 5 | 105 kg x 5 | Up |
| Bench | 70 kg x 8, 8, 7 | 70 kg x 8, 8, 8 | Up (reps) |
| Deadlift | 140 kg x 3 | 140 kg x 3 | Flat |
| Row | skipped twice | 32 kg x 10 | Incomplete |

Squat and bench earned a small next step. Deadlift needs a closer look next month, or one change now if it was already flat before week 1. The row does not have a fair comparison until the sessions exist. [How to track your workouts](/blog/how-to-track-your-workouts) is the method that makes this table possible.

## What to ignore in the monthly pass

Do not average every exercise into one score. Do not treat a deload week as a loss. If you planned a lighter week, compare the weeks on either side of it. And do not rewrite the program because a single accessory moved backward.

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
