Double progression is a simple way to know when to add weight. You pick a rep range, stay at the same weight while you add reps, and increase the weight only once you hit the top of the range on every set. Then you drop back to the bottom of the range and build up again.

## How double progression works

You progress in two steps, which is where the name comes from:

1. **Reps first.** At a fixed weight, add reps session by session until every set reaches the top of your range.
2. **Then weight.** Add the smallest weight jump available and start again near the bottom of the range.

Because the weight goes up only after you have earned it with reps, you never have to guess whether you are ready.

## How to set up double progression

1. **Choose a rep range**, for example 3 sets of 8 to 12 or 3 sets of 6 to 8.
2. **Pick a starting weight** you can lift for the bottom of the range on every set with good form and a little effort to spare.
3. **Aim to add a rep** to at least one set each session.
4. **Add weight** when every set reaches the top of the range, using the smallest jump your equipment allows.

Keep technique and range of motion the same throughout, so more reps mean real progress.

## When to add weight

The rule is simple: add weight when all sets reach the top of the range. If only one set gets there, stay at the same weight. This works especially well with dumbbells and machines, where the next weight up can be a big jump. Building extra reps first makes that jump manageable.

## An example

Illustrative numbers for a barbell row, 3 sets of 6 to 10:

| Session | Weight | Reps | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 60 kg | 7, 6, 6 | Stay |
| 2 | 60 kg | 8, 8, 7 | Stay |
| 3 | 60 kg | 9, 8, 8 | Stay |
| 4 | 60 kg | 10, 10, 10 | Add weight |
| 5 | 62.5 kg | 7, 6, 6 | Build reps again |

Reps dropping after the weight goes up is expected. The overall trend is what counts.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding weight too early**, before every set reaches the top of the range.
- **Letting form slip** to squeeze out extra reps. Cutting the range of motion makes the numbers misleading.
- **Not writing it down.** Double progression depends on knowing last session's exact reps.

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
