Overhead press strength standards estimate how much lifters at different levels can press overhead, usually as a one-rep max compared with bodyweight. Most assume a strict standing press with no leg drive. Define a valid rep, estimate your max from a recent hard set, and track your own numbers over time.

## What counts as a valid overhead press rep

For the [overhead press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_press), a strict rep usually means:

- You start with the bar at shoulder height.
- Your knees stay straight, with no dip or leg drive.
- You press until your arms are locked out overhead.
- You avoid leaning far back to finish the rep.

A push press uses the legs and allows more weight. Log it separately.

## Standing, seated, and dumbbell presses

Seated presses, dumbbell presses, and machine presses all produce different numbers. Pick one main version and keep it consistent. Your history only makes sense if the lift stays the same.

## How to estimate your overhead press

Use a recent strict set of 10 reps or fewer and a formula such as Epley or Brzycki. The method is in [how to calculate your one rep max](/blog/how-to-calculate-one-rep-max).

## Using overhead press strength standards

Tables vary by source, and the press is a lift where small differences in technique change the number. Use any table as a rough guide and track your own bodyweight ratio. See [strength standards](/blog/strength-standards) for how to read them.

## What moves the overhead press

1. **Tiny jumps.** The press often stalls with 2.5 kg jumps. Smaller increments, or adding reps first with [double progression](/blog/double-progression), help.
2. **Regular practice.** Pressing once or twice a week, as in [5x5](/blog/5x5-workout-program) or an [upper/lower split](/blog/upper-lower-split).
3. **Upper back and triceps work.** Rows and pushdowns support the lift.
4. **A tight setup.** Squeeze your glutes and keep your torso steady.

## An example

Illustrative log for a lifter weighing 70 kg:

| Month | Best set | Estimated 1RM (Epley) | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 35 kg x 6 | 42 kg | 0.60 |
| 4 | 40 kg x 6 | 48 kg | 0.69 |

A 6 kg rise in the estimate over three months is normal progress for a slow-moving lift.

## Press safely

Keep the bar path close to your face and stop the set when form changes. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
