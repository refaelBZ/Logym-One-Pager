A strength plateau is when a lift stops improving for several weeks even though you are training consistently. To break through one, first confirm it in your log, then change one thing at a time: the rep range, the weight jump, the number of sets, or a short deload. Most plateaus come from a plan that has run its course, not from a lack of effort.

## How to tell if you have hit a strength plateau

A single bad session is not a plateau. Look for:

- The same weight and reps, or lower, for three or more sessions in a row.
- Consistent attendance during that time.
- No obvious explanation, such as missed weeks or a new exercise.

If several lifts are dropping at once, it may be accumulated fatigue rather than a true plateau. See [fatigue vs plateau](/blog/fatigue-vs-plateau).

## Common causes

1. **The progression rule stopped working.** Adding weight every session cannot last forever. See [linear vs double progression](/blog/linear-vs-double-progression).
2. **Weight jumps are too big.** A 5 kg jump on a press can stall you for weeks.
3. **Inconsistent training.** Missed sessions break the pattern.
4. **Changing too much.** New exercises and rep ranges every week leave no clear trend.

[Why am I not getting stronger](/blog/why-am-i-not-getting-stronger) covers a checklist of causes.

## How to break a strength plateau

- **Switch to a rep range.** Move from a fixed 3x5 to 3 sets of 5 to 8 and use [double progression](/blog/double-progression).
- **Use smaller jumps.** Add the smallest increment your gym has.
- **Adjust volume.** Add one set, or trim sets if sessions feel draining.
- **Take a deload.** A lighter week, then build back up. See [how to deload](/blog/how-to-deload).
- **Reset the weight.** Drop about 10 percent and work back up with clean reps.

Change one variable, give it two to four weeks, and check your log.

## An example

Illustrative bench numbers before and after a change:

| Week | Plan | Best set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | 3x5 at 70 kg | 5, 4, 4 every week |
| 4 | Switch to 3x5 to 8 at 65 kg | 8, 7, 6 |
| 6 | 3x5 to 8 at 67.5 kg | 8, 7, 7 |
| 8 | 3x5 to 8 at 70 kg | 7, 6, 6 |

At week 8, 70 kg moved for more reps than before. The plateau ended without a complete program change.

## Missed reps are part of it

Missing reps now and then is normal. [What to do when you miss reps](/blog/what-to-do-when-you-miss-reps) explains how to respond. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
