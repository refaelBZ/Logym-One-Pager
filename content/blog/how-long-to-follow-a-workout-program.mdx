How long should you follow a workout program? Stay with it as long as your main lifts keep improving. For many lifters that means at least 8 to 12 weeks, and often longer for beginners. Switch when progress has stopped for a few weeks despite consistent training, or when your schedule changes. Familiarity alone is not a reason to switch.

## Why sticking with a program matters

Progress comes from repeating the same exercises and adding reps or weight over time. If you change programs every few weeks, you keep restarting and never build a clear history. A program you follow for months lets you see exactly what is working in your log.

## How long to follow a workout program: signs to keep going

- Weight or reps are rising on most main lifts.
- You are completing most planned sessions.
- The program fits your week.

If these are true, there is no reason to switch.

## Signs it may be time to change

1. **Main lifts have stalled for several weeks** even with consistent sessions and good effort. See [strength plateaus](/blog/strength-plateaus).
2. **Your schedule changed**, and the number of training days no longer fits. See [how to choose a workout split](/blog/how-to-choose-a-workout-split).
3. **You keep skipping it.** A program you do not follow is not working.

Before switching completely, try a smaller change, such as adjusting the rep range for one lift. The guide on [how to customize a workout plan](/blog/how-to-customize-a-workout-plan) covers this.

## Boredom is not a reason to restart

Feeling bored is common after a few weeks. Instead of a full change, you can swap one accessory exercise for a similar one and keep the main lifts the same. That keeps your history intact for the lifts that matter most.

## An example

An illustrative 12-week log for a squat:

| Weeks | Best set | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 4 | 60 to 70 kg x 5 | Rising |
| 5 to 8 | 72.5 to 80 kg x 5 | Rising |
| 9 to 12 | 80 kg x 5, 4 | Stalled |

Weeks 1 to 8 showed steady progress, so the program was working. A stall after week 8 is a reason to adjust, such as trying a [deload](/blog/how-to-deload) or a different rep range, rather than starting over from scratch. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.

## Use your log to decide

The answer comes from your numbers, not the calendar. A [weekly review](/blog/how-to-review-your-training-week) makes it easy to see when a program has stopped working.
