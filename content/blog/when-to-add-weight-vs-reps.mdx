When should you add weight in the gym, and when should you add reps? A simple rule works for most lifters: add reps while you are inside your target rep range, and add weight once you complete the top of that range on every set with good form. Then drop back to the bottom of the range at the new weight and build up again.

## The short rule

- **Below the top of your range on any set?** Keep the weight and try for one more rep.
- **At the top of your range on all sets?** Add the smallest weight jump available.
- **Form slipping to get the reps?** Keep the weight until the reps look clean.

This is the core of [double progression](/blog/double-progression), and it removes guesswork from every session. The free [double progression chart](/tools/double-progression-chart) shows whether the next session calls for a rep or a weight jump.

## When to add weight in the gym

Add weight when the current load has become comfortable for all your planned reps. Signs it is time:

1. You hit the top of the range on every set, including the last one.
2. The last rep looks like the first rep.
3. You did it in your normal rest time, not after an extra-long break.

Use the smallest jump your equipment allows. Small jumps keep you inside your rep range after the increase.

## When to add reps instead

Add reps when a weight increase would be too big a step. This happens often with dumbbells and some machines, where the next weight up can feel like a large jump. Adding a rep or two at the same weight builds toward that jump. It also makes sense after a weight increase, when your reps drop and you need to rebuild them. For more on that situation, see [progressive overload with dumbbells](/blog/progressive-overload-with-dumbbells).

## An example

Illustrative numbers for a lat pulldown, 3 sets of 8 to 12:

| Session | Weight | Reps | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 45 kg | 10, 9, 9 | Add reps |
| 2 | 45 kg | 12, 11, 10 | Add reps |
| 3 | 45 kg | 12, 12, 12 | Add weight |
| 4 | 50 kg | 9, 8, 8 | Add reps |

Session 4 has fewer reps than session 3. That is expected after a jump, and the plan is simply to build back up.

## Common mistakes

- Adding weight because one set reached the top of the range.
- Adding reps by cutting depth or range of motion.
- Changing the weight, rest, and exercise all at once, so you cannot tell what worked.

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
