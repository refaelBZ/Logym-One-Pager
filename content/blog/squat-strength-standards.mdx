Squat strength standards estimate how much lifters at different levels can squat, usually as a one-rep max compared with bodyweight. The biggest variable in any squat number is depth, so decide what a valid rep looks like first. Then estimate your max from a recent hard set and track it over time against your own history.

## What counts as a valid squat rep

For the [squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squat_(exercise)), a common standard for a full rep is:

- Your hip crease drops to or below the top of your knee.
- You stand all the way back up with hips and knees locked.
- The bar stays on your back and your feet stay planted.

Many standards assume this depth. A shallower squat will look stronger on paper but is not comparable.

## Bar position and variations

High-bar, low-bar, front squats, and box squats are different lifts with different numbers. Log each as its own exercise. Changing bar position mid-program makes your history hard to read.

## How to estimate your squat

Take a recent hard set of 10 reps or fewer at full depth and use a formula such as Epley or Brzycki. [How to calculate your one rep max](/blog/how-to-calculate-one-rep-max) walks through it. Use the same formula each time.

## Using squat strength standards

Different tables give different numbers, so treat any one as a rough guide. A personal bodyweight ratio is easy to follow: estimated 1RM divided by bodyweight. For context and limits, read [strength standards](/blog/strength-standards).

## What moves the squat

1. **Consistent depth.** Film a set now and then to check.
2. **Steady overload.** Small weight jumps when all reps are done. Beginners often use [linear progression](/blog/linear-vs-double-progression).
3. **Frequency.** Squatting two or three times a week, as in [5x5](/blog/5x5-workout-program) or full body plans, gives more practice.
4. **Supporting leg work.** [Lunges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunge_(exercise)), Romanian deadlifts, and leg presses help.

## An example

Illustrative log for a lifter weighing 80 kg:

| Month | Best set | Estimated 1RM (Epley) | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 80 kg x 5 | 93 kg | 1.17 |
| 4 | 95 kg x 5 | 111 kg | 1.39 |

The same depth standard was used throughout, so the gain is real.

## Squat safely

Set safety bars or pins just below your bottom position, and use them on heavy sets. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
