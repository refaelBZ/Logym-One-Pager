An upper lower split alternates upper body days and lower body days. The most common version runs four days a week: two upper sessions and two lower sessions, so every muscle group is trained twice a week. It is a popular next step after full body training. A four-day layout is in the free [workout templates](/tools/workout-templates).

## How an upper lower split works

| Day | Focus | Example exercises |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper A | Strength focus | [Bench press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_press), [bent-over row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bent-over_row), overhead press |
| Lower A | Strength focus | [Squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squat_(exercise)), [Romanian deadlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_deadlift), calf raise |
| Upper B | Higher reps | Incline dumbbell press, pulldown, lateral raise |
| Lower B | Higher reps | [Deadlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlift) or leg press, lunge, leg curl |

Using an A and B version of each day lets you train main lifts heavier once a week and use more reps with variations on the other day.

## How to schedule it

A common week is Monday upper, Tuesday lower, Thursday upper, Friday lower, with rest on the other days. Any schedule with a rest day after every two sessions works. If you can only make three days, you can rotate through the four sessions in order, so each week starts where the last one ended.

## How to progress

1. Use 3 to 4 sets per exercise.
2. Pick a rep range, such as 5 to 8 for main lifts and 8 to 12 for accessories.
3. Add reps until you reach the top of the range, then add weight. See [when to add weight vs reps](/blog/when-to-add-weight-vs-reps).
4. Log every set so you can beat last week's numbers.

Keep the same exercises on each day for several weeks so your log shows a clear trend.

## Who it suits

An upper lower split suits lifters who can train four days a week and want more volume per muscle than a full body plan allows. It is easier to recover between sessions than six days of [push pull legs](/blog/push-pull-legs-program). Beginners can start here or with a [full body plan](/blog/full-body-workout-plan-for-beginners). For more options, see [how to choose a workout split](/blog/how-to-choose-a-workout-split).

## An example

Illustrative bench numbers across two weeks on Upper A, 3 sets of 5 to 8:

| Week | Weight | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 60 kg | 7, 6, 6 |
| 2 | 60 kg | 8, 7, 7 |

Reps rose at the same weight, so the plan is on track. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
