Progressive overload with dumbbells means making your dumbbell exercises a little harder over time. Because the jump between dumbbells is often bigger than the jump on a barbell, the most practical approach is to add reps within a rep range first, and move up to the next dumbbell only when you reach the top of that range on every set.

## Why dumbbells need a different approach

On a barbell you can usually add a small amount at a time. Dumbbells often go up in fixed steps, such as 2 kg or 2.5 kg per hand. For a lighter dumbbell, that can be a large share of the weight you are lifting. Going straight to the next pair often means your reps drop sharply. Building extra reps first makes the jump manageable.

## How to apply progressive overload with dumbbells

1. **Pick a wide rep range**, such as 8 to 15 for presses and rows, or 10 to 20 for smaller exercises like lateral raises.
2. **Add reps** each session at the same weight.
3. **Move up** to the next dumbbell when every set reaches the top of the range.
4. **Expect your reps to drop** after the jump, then build back up.

This is [double progression](/blog/double-progression) with a wider range to absorb bigger weight jumps.

## Other ways to progress with dumbbells

When you cannot add weight yet, you still have options:

- **Add a set** of the same weight and reps.
- **Slow the lowering phase** in a consistent way, and keep it the same each session so the numbers stay comparable.
- **Switch to a harder variation**, such as moving from a seated to a standing press.

Change one thing at a time so your log shows what made the difference.

## An example

Illustrative numbers for a dumbbell row, 3 sets of 8 to 15:

| Session | Weight per hand | Reps | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 20 kg | 10, 9, 9 | Add reps |
| 2 | 20 kg | 12, 11, 11 | Add reps |
| 3 | 20 kg | 15, 14, 13 | Add reps |
| 4 | 20 kg | 15, 15, 15 | Move to 22.5 kg |
| 5 | 22.5 kg | 10, 9, 9 | Build reps again |

The wide range gave room to grow before the jump. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
