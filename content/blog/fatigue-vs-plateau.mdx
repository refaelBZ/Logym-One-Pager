Fatigue vs plateau: if several lifts drop at once and sessions feel harder than usual, accumulated training fatigue is the more likely cause, and a lighter week often helps. If one lift stays stuck for weeks while you feel fresh and other lifts improve, that points to a plateau, which usually needs a change to the plan for that lift.

## Signs of training fatigue

- Numbers dropping on several lifts in the same week.
- Warm-up weights feeling heavier than normal.
- Sets that used to be moderate now feel close to failure.
- It started after a period of more sets, more sessions, or harder training.

## Signs of a true plateau

- One lift stuck at the same weight and reps for three or more sessions.
- Other lifts still progressing.
- You feel normal in sessions.
- You have been following the same progression rule for a long time.

## Fatigue vs plateau at a glance

| | Fatigue | Plateau |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lifts affected | Many at once | Usually one or two |
| How sessions feel | Harder than usual | Normal |
| Recent changes | Often more training | Often none |
| Typical response | Lighter week | Change the lift's plan |

## How to respond to fatigue

Take a planned lighter week. Keep your exercises and cut sets or weight. The steps are in [how to deload](/blog/how-to-deload). Then return to normal training and see whether the numbers come back.

## How to respond to a plateau

Change one thing for the stuck lift: switch to a rep range, use smaller weight jumps, or adjust the number of sets. [Strength plateaus](/blog/strength-plateaus) covers each option.

## An example

Illustrative two-week snapshot:

| Lift | Week 1 | Week 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 80 kg x 5, 5, 5 | 80 kg x 5, 4, 3 |
| Bench | 60 kg x 8, 8, 8 | 60 kg x 7, 6, 6 |
| Row | 50 kg x 10, 10, 10 | 50 kg x 9, 8, 8 |

All three dropped in the same week after extra sets were added. That pattern suggests fatigue rather than a plateau, so a lighter week is the first thing to try.

## When it is neither

If you feel unwell, or a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional. Training changes are not the answer in those cases.
