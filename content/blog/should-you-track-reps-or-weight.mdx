Track both reps and weight. Each number is incomplete alone. Weight tells you the load on the bar. Reps tell you how much work you did with it. Progress hides when a log keeps only one of them.

## When to watch reps or weight more closely

You still write both down every set. The number you try to beat depends on the rule you are using this week.

- **Inside a rep range, reps are the target.** If the plan is 3 sets of 6 to 10 at 24 kg, you are done raising the load only after the sets reach 10. Until then, a jump from 7 reps to 9 reps is the progress. [Double progression](/blog/double-progression) is built on that order.
- **At the top of the range, weight is the target.** Once every set hits the top, add the smallest plate you can and let the reps fall back toward the bottom of the range. The new line in the log should show the heavier load, even if the reps drop.
- **On a fixed-rep plan, weight is the target and reps are the check.** A 5x5 entry of "60 kg" means little if two sets stopped at 3. Write 5, 5, 5, 4, 3 so you know the weight was not earned yet.

[When to add weight vs reps](/blog/when-to-add-weight-vs-reps) is the decision rule. This page is about what the log must contain so that decision is obvious next time.

## Why one column misleads you

| What you wrote | What actually happened | What you might think |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 kg | 50 kg for 6, then 4 | The weight is "your" 50 kg |
| 8 reps | 20 kg last month, 24 kg now | Reps stalled |
| 3x8 | Third set was 5 | The session matched the plan |

[How to track progressive overload](/blog/how-to-track-progressive-overload) uses the full line, weight times reps, for this reason.

## A short worked week

Lat pulldown, range of 8 to 12:

- Week 1: 40 kg x 12, 11, 9. Reps are still the target on the later sets.
- Week 2: 40 kg x 12, 12, 12. The range is full, so next time the weight moves.
- Week 3: 42.5 kg x 10, 9, 8. Reps fell, the load rose, and both facts belong in the log.

If a set causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
