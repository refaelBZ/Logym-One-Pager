Measure strength progress from the training log: the weight on the bar, the reps you completed, the sets you finished, and how many planned sessions you actually did. A bathroom scale can rise or fall for reasons that have nothing to do with whether your squat improved this month. For lifting, the session record is the measurement.

## How to measure strength progress from a log

Use four checks, in this order:

1. **Load and reps on the main lifts.** Same exercise, same rep target, heavier bar: stronger. Same bar, more reps: stronger. Write both numbers every time. [How to track training volume](/blog/how-to-track-training-volume) helps when you want every set added up, including the sets that were not your best.
2. **Personal records, used sparingly.** A new best set is a milestone. It is a poor weekly score because most good weeks do not include one. [How to track personal records](/blog/how-to-track-personal-records) keeps those milestones from getting lost.
3. **Sessions completed.** Three productive weeks out of four beat a single huge day followed by skipped workouts. Count attendance before you judge a lift. The free [weekly training review](/tools/weekly-training-review) is a short way to run that check.
4. **A rough standard, as context only.** [Strength standards](/blog/strength-standards) compare a lift with body weight as a population estimate. They are a map, not a score you must hit on a deadline, and they do not replace your own previous sessions.

## What the scale cannot tell you

Two lifters can weigh the same and bench very different loads. The same lifter can weigh the same in March and June while every working set has gone up. The reverse happens too: body weight changes and the bar does not. If your goal is a stronger lift, track the lift.

This is not a guide to diet or to changing body weight. Those topics sit outside a training log.

## A simple month scorecard

Illustrative notes you can copy into the back of a log:

| Check | Week 1 | Week 4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat best set | 90 kg x 5 | 95 kg x 5 |
| Bench sets at 60 kg | 8, 7, 6 | 8, 8, 8 |
| Sessions done | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 |

That scorecard shows stronger squat sets, a fuller bench, and steady attendance. No scale reading is required.

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
