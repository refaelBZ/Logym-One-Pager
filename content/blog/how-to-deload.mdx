To deload, train for one week with the same exercises but less work: either lower the weight noticeably or cut your sets roughly in half, and keep every set comfortably short of failure. Then return to your normal plan the following week. A deload is a planned lighter week that can help you start the next block feeling fresh.

## When to consider a deload

- Several lifts have been flat or dropping for a couple of weeks.
- Sessions feel harder than the numbers suggest.
- You have finished a long, hard training block.

Some programs schedule a deload every few weeks. Others use one only when progress stalls. Both approaches are common. If you are unsure whether you are stalled or just tired, read [fatigue vs plateau](/blog/fatigue-vs-plateau).

## How to deload: three simple options

| Option | What changes | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fewer sets | Same weight, about half the sets | 4 sets becomes 2 |
| Lighter weight | Same sets, lower weight | 80 kg becomes about 60 kg |
| Both, mildly | Slightly fewer sets and slightly lighter | 4x80 kg becomes 3x70 kg |

Pick one option and keep it simple. Keep the same exercises so you stay practiced on them.

## How to run the week

1. **Keep your normal training days.** The routine stays the same.
2. **Warm up as usual.**
3. **Do your deload sets** and stop well before any rep gets hard.
4. **Log everything**, marked as a deload, so it does not look like lost strength later.

## Coming back after a deload

Return to your previous working weights, or slightly below them, the week after. Many lifters find their lifts move again soon after. If they do not, look at the bigger picture in [strength plateaus](/blog/strength-plateaus).

## An example

Illustrative squat numbers around a deload week:

| Week | Sets | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 80 kg x 5, 5, 4 | Stalled |
| 7 | 80 kg x 5, 5 | Deload: half the sets |
| 8 | 80 kg x 5, 5, 5 | Back to normal |
| 9 | 82.5 kg x 5, 5, 5 | Progressing |

The deload week had fewer sets, and the following weeks moved forward again. Results vary, so check your own log.

## What a deload is not

A deload is not a week off and not a chance to test maxes. It is lighter training on your normal schedule. If you feel pain or unwell, stop and consult a qualified professional.
