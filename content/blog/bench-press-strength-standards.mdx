Bench press strength standards estimate how much lifters at different levels can bench, usually as a one-rep max compared with bodyweight. They are useful only if your bench is measured the same way each time. Start by defining a valid rep, estimate your max from a recent hard set, and track that number against your own history.

## What counts as a valid bench press rep

Standards assume a full rep. For the [bench press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_press), that usually means:

- The bar touches your chest on the way down.
- Your hips stay on the bench.
- Your arms lock out at the top.
- No bouncing the bar off your chest.

Some lifters also pause briefly on the chest. A paused bench is harder, so log it as a separate exercise.

## How to estimate your bench press

You do not need to test a true max. Take a recent hard set of 10 reps or fewer and use a formula such as Epley. The steps are in [how to calculate your one rep max](/blog/how-to-calculate-one-rep-max). Always use the same formula so estimates stay comparable.

## Using bench press strength standards

Different tables give different numbers, so treat any single table as a rough guide. A simple personal measure is a bodyweight ratio: estimated 1RM divided by bodyweight. Track it every few months. For the general picture, see [strength standards](/blog/strength-standards).

## What moves the bench

1. **Consistent technique.** Same grip width, same touch point, same setup.
2. **Gradual overload.** Small weight jumps or extra reps. The bench often moves in small steps, so [double progression](/blog/double-progression) works well.
3. **Upper body pulling and pressing work.** Rows, overhead presses, and dips or push-ups support the lift.
4. **Enough rest between heavy sets.** See [how long to rest between sets](/blog/how-long-to-rest-between-sets).

## An example

Illustrative log for a lifter weighing 75 kg:

| Month | Best set | Estimated 1RM (Epley) | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 60 kg x 6 | 72 kg | 0.96 |
| 3 | 65 kg x 6 | 78 kg | 1.04 |

The estimate rose by 6 kg, and the ratio passed bodyweight. That progress matters whatever a table says.

## Bench safely

Use a spotter or safety arms for heavy sets, and never bench to failure alone without safeties. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
