A push pull legs program divides your training into three session types: push days for chest, shoulders, and triceps, pull days for back and biceps, and leg days for the lower body. You can run it 3 days a week, doing each session once, or 6 days a week, doing each twice. Sample sessions are in the free [workout templates](/tools/workout-templates).

## How a push pull legs program works

| Day | Focus | Example exercises |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Push | Chest, shoulders, triceps | [Bench press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_press), overhead press, triceps pushdown |
| Pull | Back, biceps | Pull-up or pulldown, row, biceps curl |
| Legs | Quads, hamstrings, glutes | [Squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squat_(exercise)), [Romanian deadlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_deadlift), lunge |

Grouping by movement keeps sessions focused, and muscles that work together are trained on the same day.

## 3-day vs 6-day push pull legs

**3 days:** each muscle group is trained once a week. Sessions can be longer. This suits people with limited time, though full body or upper/lower splits train each muscle more often on the same schedule.

**6 days:** each session runs twice a week. It needs a lot of gym time and good consistency. A missed day shifts the whole week.

Many lifters land in between, with 4 or 5 days rotating through the sequence. See [3-day vs 4-day vs 5-day splits](/blog/3-day-vs-4-day-vs-5-day-split).

## How to set up each session

1. Start with one main compound lift.
2. Add two or three accessory exercises.
3. Use 3 to 4 sets per exercise and a rep range, such as 6 to 10 for the main lift and 8 to 15 for accessories.
4. Progress with [double progression](/blog/double-progression): add reps, then weight.

## An example

An illustrative 6-day week:

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Push | Pull | Legs | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest |

On the 3-day version, the same week would be Push Monday, Pull Wednesday, Legs Friday.

## Common mistakes

- Letting leg day slip when the week gets busy.
- Adding too many exercises, so sessions run long.
- Changing exercises every week, which leaves no clear history to beat.

## Who it suits

Push pull legs suits lifters who enjoy focused sessions and can train often. Beginners usually do well starting with [full body workouts](/blog/full-body-workout-plan-for-beginners) and moving to push pull legs later. For help choosing, read [how to choose a workout split](/blog/how-to-choose-a-workout-split). If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
