If you are wondering what to log in a workout, start with four things for every working set: the exercise, the weight, the reps you completed, and the date. Those details let you repeat a session and try to beat it. Most other fields are optional, and some just slow you down.

## The four things to log every time

- **Exercise.** Write the exact variation, such as "barbell back squat" or "incline dumbbell press", and use the same name every session.
- **Weight.** Stick to one unit. For dumbbells, note whether the number is per hand.
- **Reps.** Record what you actually did on each set, not what the plan said.
- **Date or session name.** If you rotate workouts like "Upper A" and "Lower B", write which one it was.

Log each set on its own. "3x8" hides the difference between 8, 8, 8 and 8, 7, 5, and that difference tells you whether to add weight next time.

## Worth adding when it helps

Some details earn a place in your log because they change how you read the numbers:

- **A short note** when something is different: another machine, a new grip, a different bar, or a change in how deep you squat.
- **Machine settings** such as seat height, if you use more than one machine of the same type.
- **An effort rating**, for example how many more reps you think you had left. It helps you see when the same weight starts to feel easier.

## What you can skip

Skip anything you never look at again. Warm-up sets rarely change your next session, so most lifters log working sets only. Long written reflections, exact times for every set, and detailed mood scores tend to turn logging into a chore. If a field does not help you decide what to lift next time, leave it out.

## An example entry

Here is an illustrative entry with only the essentials and one note:

```text
Lower B - squat day
Barbell back squat:  80 kg x 6, 6, 5
Romanian deadlift:   70 kg x 8, 8, 8
Leg press (seat 4):  120 kg x 10, 10, 9
Note: squats with a new belt
```

Next time, the squat target is 6 reps on the last set. The note explains why this session might not compare cleanly with the last one.

## Keep it fast

The best log is the one you fill in every session. Write each set during your rest, while the numbers are fresh.
