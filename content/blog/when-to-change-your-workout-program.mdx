Change your workout program when the main lifts stay flat for several weeks after you have already tried one small fix, or when you repeatedly cannot complete the sessions as written. Change it later than your impatience suggests. A new plan wipes the history you need to judge the next one.

[How long to follow a workout program](/blog/how-long-to-follow-a-workout-program) covers a full block, often around twelve weeks. This page is the shorter list of reasons to leave earlier, and the reasons that feel urgent but are not.

## When to change your workout program

These are reasonable triggers:

- **A stall that survived a tweak.** You logged the same weight and reps for several sessions, tried one change such as a smaller jump or a rep range, waited two to four weeks, and the lift is still flat. [Strength plateaus](/blog/strength-plateaus) explains that sequence. A new program is the step after it, not the first step.
- **The schedule does not fit your week.** If you miss the same day every week, a plan with fewer days will produce more progress than a harder plan you skip.
- **You have no comparable history.** The exercises change every session, so you cannot tell whether you are stronger. At that point the "program" is not giving you a way to progress. Replace it with a short list of lifts you will repeat.

Small edits are not a new program. [How to customize a workout plan](/blog/how-to-customize-a-workout-plan) is the right size of change when one exercise needs a swap and the rest is fine.

## When to stay

Stay if:

- One week felt boring or heavy.
- A single lift dipped and the others rose.
- You are in a planned lighter week.
- You have been on the plan for only a couple of weeks and the weights are still going up.

Boredom is a real feeling. It is a weak signal compared with a log that is still climbing. Skipping ahead to a new split because the current one is familiar often means restarting at lighter weights and calling that progress.

## A decision you can write down

After the monthly look at your log, finish one sentence: "I will keep this plan until ___, unless ___ is still flat." A date and a lift name beat a vague plan to "switch soon."

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional before you design the next block around it.
