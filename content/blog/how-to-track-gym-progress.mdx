To track gym progress, write down the weight and the completed reps for every working set, then compare that exercise with the last time you trained it. One hard session does not show a trend. A few weeks of the same lifts do. That record is the simplest way to see whether [strength training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_training) is moving forward. The free [weekly training review](/tools/weekly-training-review) turns those weeks into a short check.

## What to record so you can track gym progress

Keep the list short enough that you will fill it in between sets:

- Exercise name, written the same way each time.
- Weight used.
- Reps you actually completed, not the reps you planned.
- The date, so weeks line up later.

Optional notes help when a number looks odd: a shorter rest, a different bar, or a set you stopped early. Skip long comments. The guide to [what to log in a workout](/blog/what-to-log-in-a-workout) covers which extras are worth keeping.

## Compare the same lift, not the whole session

A session total is hard to read. Bench might go up while a row stays flat, and a single "good workout" label hides that. Compare each main lift with its previous entry.

Look for one of these changes:

1. More reps at the same weight.
2. The same reps at a heavier weight.
3. An extra set at a weight you already handle cleanly.

Any one of those is progress. [Progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) is the name for making training a little harder over time, and the log is how you know the next step.

## A four-week example

Illustrative dumbbell row numbers, per hand, best set listed last:

| Week | Weight | Sets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 22 kg | 8, 8, 7 |
| 2 | 22 kg | 8, 8, 8 |
| 3 | 22 kg | 9, 8, 8 |
| 4 | 24 kg | 8, 7, 7 |

Week 4 is lighter on reps because the weight went up. The log shows that trade clearly. Without it, week 4 can feel like a step backward.

## When the numbers stop moving

If the same weight and reps repeat for several sessions, you have a stall to look at, not a reason to abandon the log. Check attendance first, then the size of your weight jumps. [How to track progressive overload](/blog/how-to-track-progressive-overload) walks through the columns that make that check fast.

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
