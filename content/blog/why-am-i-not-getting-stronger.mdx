Why am I not getting stronger? In most cases the answer is one of a few training habits: you are not training consistently, you have no clear rule for adding weight or reps, you change your plan too often, or you are not tracking your sets. Work through the checklist below to find the most likely cause.

## The checklist

### 1. Are you training consistently?

Strength builds from repeated sessions. If you train two weeks on and one week off, progress stalls. Check your log: how many planned sessions did you complete last month? [How often should beginners work out](/blog/how-often-should-beginners-work-out) helps set a realistic target.

### 2. Do you have a progression rule?

Without a rule, the weights tend to stay the same. Pick one, such as [double progression](/blog/double-progression), and apply it to every main lift. [When to add weight vs reps](/blog/when-to-add-weight-vs-reps) makes the decision simple.

### 3. Are you changing too much?

New exercises every week mean you never build a history on any of them. Keep your main lifts for at least several weeks. See [how long to follow a workout program](/blog/how-long-to-follow-a-workout-program).

### 4. Are you tracking your sets?

If you do not know last week's numbers, you cannot reliably beat them. Logging takes seconds. Start with [how to log sets during your workout](/blog/how-to-log-sets-during-your-workout).

### 5. Are your sets hard enough?

Sets that end with many reps left may not push progress. Rating effort with [RPE or RIR](/blog/rpe-vs-rir) shows how close you are getting.

### 6. Are you resting enough between sets?

Very short rest can cut your reps on heavy lifts. See [how long to rest between sets](/blog/how-long-to-rest-between-sets).

## An example

An illustrative case: a lifter feels stuck on the squat. The log shows three sessions in the last month instead of eight, and a different rep target each time. The fix is not a new program. It is two squat sessions a week with one rep range and a clear rule for adding weight. Within a few weeks, reps start climbing.

| Check | Finding | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consistency | 3 of 8 sessions | Fixed training days |
| Progression | No rule | Double progression |
| Tracking | Partial | Log every set |

## If everything checks out

If you train consistently, follow a progression rule, and still stall for weeks, you may have reached a true plateau. [Strength plateaus](/blog/strength-plateaus) and [how to deload](/blog/how-to-deload) cover the next steps. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
