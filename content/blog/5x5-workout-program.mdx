A 5x5 workout program is a simple strength plan built on 5 sets of 5 reps for a few big barbell lifts. A common version alternates two workouts three days a week, and you add a small amount of weight each time you complete all the reps. It suits beginners who want a clear, repeatable plan for the barbell basics. A ready-made version sits in the free [workout templates](/tools/workout-templates).

## How a 5x5 workout program works

Most 5x5 plans use two workouts, A and B, on non-consecutive days:

| Workout A | Workout B |
| --- | --- |
| [Squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squat_(exercise)) 5x5 | Squat 5x5 |
| [Bench press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_press) 5x5 | [Overhead press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_press) 5x5 |
| [Bent-over row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bent-over_row) 5x5 | [Deadlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlift) 1x5 |

You alternate A and B, so one week is A, B, A and the next is B, A, B. Many versions use a single set of deadlifts because the lift is demanding.

## How to progress on 5x5

The plan uses [linear progression](/blog/linear-vs-double-progression):

1. Start with weights well below your limit so the first weeks feel manageable.
2. If you complete all 5 sets of 5, add a small amount next session, for example 2.5 kg. The free [plate calculator](/tools/plate-calculator) shows which plates make that bar weight.
3. If you miss reps, repeat the same weight next time.
4. If you miss the same weight several sessions in a row, lower it a little and build back up.

## Who 5x5 suits

5x5 works well for lifters who are new to barbell training, can train three days a week, and like a fixed plan. It is less suited to people who prefer higher reps, do not have access to a barbell and rack, or want many exercises per session. For other options, see [how to choose a workout split](/blog/how-to-choose-a-workout-split).

## An example

Illustrative first three sessions:

| Session | Squat | Press | Pull |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (A) | 40 kg 5x5 | Bench 30 kg 5x5 | Row 30 kg 5x5 |
| 2 (B) | 42.5 kg 5x5 | OHP 20 kg 5x5 | Deadlift 50 kg 1x5 |
| 3 (A) | 45 kg 5x5 | Bench 32.5 kg 5x5 | Row 32.5 kg 5x5 |

Squats go up every session because they appear in both workouts.

## Tips for running it

- Learn the lifts with light weights first.
- Rest long enough between heavy sets; see [how long to rest between sets](/blog/how-long-to-rest-between-sets).
- Log every set, so you know the exact weight for next session.

If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
