RPE vs RIR is a question of how you describe effort. RPE (rating of perceived exertion) scores a set from 1 to 10 based on how hard it felt. RIR (reps in reserve) counts how many more good reps you could have done. In strength training the two map neatly: RPE 10 means 0 reps left, RPE 9 means 1 left, and RPE 8 means about 2 left.

## What RIR means

After a set, ask: "How many more clean reps could I have done?" If the answer is two, the set was 2 RIR. It is concrete and easy for beginners, because you are counting reps rather than rating a feeling.

## What RPE means

RPE puts the same idea on a 1 to 10 scale. Lifters usually use only the top of the scale, from about 6 to 10. Half points like 8.5 mean "maybe 2 reps left, definitely 1". Wikipedia has background on the general [rating of perceived exertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_of_perceived_exertion) idea.

## RPE vs RIR conversion

| RPE | RIR | What it feels like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 0 | No more reps possible |
| 9 | 1 | One more rep left |
| 8 | 2 | Two more reps left |
| 7 | 3 | Three more reps left |
| 6 | 4 or more | Moderate, many reps left |

## Which one to use

Use RIR if you are newer to lifting or want the simplest option. Use RPE if your plan is written in RPE, or you like half-point detail. They describe the same thing, so pick one and stay consistent. Mixing scales in the same log makes it harder to compare sessions.

## How to log effort

1. Finish the set and rack the weight.
2. Ask how many good reps were left.
3. Write the number next to weight and reps, for example "80 kg x 5 @ 2 RIR".

Ratings get more accurate with practice. Early on, many lifters underestimate how many reps they have left, so treat your first ratings as rough.

## An example

Illustrative squat sets with a target of 2 RIR:

| Set | Weight | Reps | RIR | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 80 kg | 5 | 3 | Fine, keep going |
| 2 | 80 kg | 5 | 2 | On target |
| 3 | 80 kg | 5 | 1 | Harder than planned |

If set 3 lands at 1 RIR again next week, keep the weight. If all sets feel like 3 RIR, add weight. This pairs well with [when to add weight vs reps](/blog/when-to-add-weight-vs-reps). If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.

## Why effort ratings help your log

Weight and reps show what you did. Effort shows how hard it was. Together they explain whether a repeated number was easy or a grind, which matters when you [review your training week](/blog/how-to-review-your-training-week).
