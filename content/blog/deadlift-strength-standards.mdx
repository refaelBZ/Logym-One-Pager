Deadlift strength standards estimate how much lifters at different levels can deadlift, usually as a one-rep max compared with bodyweight. To use them, you need a consistent definition of a valid rep, an honest estimate of your max, and a log that tracks it over months. Your own trend is the most useful standard.

## What counts as a valid deadlift rep

For the [deadlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlift), a full rep usually means:

- The bar starts from a dead stop on the floor.
- You stand fully upright with hips and knees locked out.
- No hitching the bar up your thighs.
- You lower the bar under control.

Each rep should start from the floor. Bouncing reps off the plates make a set look easier than it is.

## Stance and variations

Conventional and sumo deadlifts use different stances and often produce different numbers. Trap bar deadlifts, [Romanian deadlifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_deadlift), and deficit pulls are separate exercises too. Log each under its own name.

## How to estimate your deadlift

Use a recent hard set of 10 reps or fewer and a formula such as Epley. Many lifters prefer sets of 3 to 5 for the deadlift because form can slip in long sets. [How to calculate your one rep max](/blog/how-to-calculate-one-rep-max) explains the math.

## Using deadlift strength standards

Different tables give different numbers, and grip, stance, and limb length all affect your pull. Treat tables as a rough guide and track a bodyweight ratio for yourself. See [strength standards](/blog/strength-standards) for the broader picture.

## What moves the deadlift

1. **A repeatable setup.** Same foot position, grip, and bar path every rep.
2. **Low reps with good form.** Many programs use one or a few work sets, as in [5x5](/blog/5x5-workout-program).
3. **Grip.** If the bar slips before your back or legs give out, practice holding heavy weights.
4. **Patient overload.** Smaller jumps once big jumps stop working.

## An example

Illustrative log for a lifter weighing 70 kg:

| Month | Best set | Estimated 1RM (Epley) | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 90 kg x 5 | 105 kg | 1.50 |
| 4 | 110 kg x 5 | 128 kg | 1.83 |

Every rep in the log started from the floor, so the numbers are comparable.

## Pull safely

Stop a set when your form changes. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
