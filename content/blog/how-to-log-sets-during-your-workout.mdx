The easiest way to log workouts at the gym is to record each set during the rest right after it: the weight and the reps you completed. It takes a few seconds and spares you from rebuilding the session from memory at the end.

## Before your first set

Open your log and look at the last time you did this workout. For each exercise, note what you lifted and pick one target for today, such as one more rep on the final set or a small increase in weight. Walking in with a target makes every set count.

## How to log workouts at the gym, set by set

1. **Finish the set** and rack the weight.
2. **Log it right away**, while you catch your breath: weight and reps completed.
3. **Write the real result.** If you planned 8 reps and got 6, log 6.
4. **Check the next set's target** before the rest period ends.

Logging during the rest fits into time you are already spending, so it does not lengthen your workout.

## Keep entries consistent

Small habits keep your history readable:

- **Use the same exercise name every time.** "Bench", "flat bench" and "BB bench press" split one lift into three records.
- **Stay with one unit.** If your gym mixes kilograms and pounds, convert as you log.
- **Add a quick note when something changes**, such as a different machine or a new grip.
- **Start a new entry for a new variation** instead of mixing it into an old one.

For a full list of what belongs in an entry, see [what to log in a workout](/blog/what-to-log-in-a-workout).

## What to do when things go off plan

Gyms get busy and plans change. If your usual machine is taken and you use another one, log it as that machine. If you cut a set short, log the reps you got. If you skip an exercise, leave it out rather than copying last week's numbers. An honest log is the only kind that helps you plan the next session.

## An example

An illustrative bench press log, entered set by set during rest:

| Set | Target | Logged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 60 kg x 8 | 60 kg x 8 |
| 2 | 60 kg x 8 | 60 kg x 8 |
| 3 | 60 kg x 8 | 60 kg x 7 |

Next session, the goal is clear: 8 reps on the third set. Once you hit it, the weight can go up, as explained in [how to track progressive overload](/blog/how-to-track-progressive-overload).

## After the session

Take a few seconds to glance over the entries and fix any typos while you still remember. For the bigger picture, the guide to [how to track your workouts](/blog/how-to-track-your-workouts) shows how to read your log over weeks. If an exercise causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
