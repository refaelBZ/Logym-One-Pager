Beginner strength training at the gym comes down to a few habits: follow a simple plan two or three times a week, learn a handful of basic lifts, start with weights that feel manageable, write down what you do, and add a little over time. You do not need a complicated program to start getting stronger.

## Start with a simple plan

A beginner plan should be short enough to finish and easy to repeat. Full-body workouts, where each session trains the whole body, are a common starting point because every major lift gets practiced several times a week. Two or three sessions a week with a rest day between them is plenty to begin. For a sample, see the [full body workout plan for beginners](/blog/full-body-workout-plan-for-beginners), and for scheduling, read [how often beginners should work out](/blog/how-often-should-beginners-work-out).

## Learn the main movements

Most [strength training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_training) plans are built from a few movement patterns:

- **Squat**, such as a goblet squat or a barbell back squat.
- **Hinge**, such as a Romanian deadlift.
- **Push**, such as a push-up, bench press, or overhead press.
- **Pull**, such as a dumbbell row or a lat pulldown.

Pick one exercise for each pattern and stick with it for a few weeks. Repeating the same lifts is how you get better at them and how you can see progress in your numbers.

## Choose your first weights

Start lighter than you think you need. A good first weight is one you can lift for all your planned reps with clean form and a couple of reps left in the tank. If the last rep looks or feels very different from the first, the weight is too heavy for now. If you could do many more reps, go up a little next set.

## Track every session from day one

Early progress can come quickly, and it is easy to lose track of. Write down the exercise, weight, and reps for every set. The guide to [what to log in a workout](/blog/what-to-log-in-a-workout) covers the essentials, and [how to track your workouts](/blog/how-to-track-your-workouts) shows how to use the numbers.

## Add a little each week

Once a weight feels comfortable for all your reps, add one more rep or a small amount of weight next time. That gradual increase is called progressive overload, and [progressive overload for beginners](/blog/progressive-overload-for-beginners) explains how to apply it safely in your first months.

## An example first week

Illustrative plan, three days with rest days between:

| Day | Workout |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Full body A: goblet squat, push-up, dumbbell row |
| Wednesday | Full body B: Romanian deadlift, overhead press, lat pulldown |
| Friday | Full body A again |

Do two or three sets of 8 to 12 reps per exercise, and rest long enough to do each set well. If an exercise causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
