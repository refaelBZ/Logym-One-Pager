Your first 4 weeks in the gym should be simple: train 2 to 3 full body sessions a week, learn a handful of basic exercises with light weights, and log every set. The goals for the first month are building the habit, learning good form, and finding your starting weights, not lifting as heavy as possible. A simple starting plan is in the free [workout templates](/tools/workout-templates).

## Week by week

| Week | Focus | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Learn the movements | Light weights, 2 to 3 sets per exercise |
| 2 | Find working weights | Add weight until sets feel challenging with good form |
| 3 | Start progressing | Add a rep or a small amount of weight |
| 4 | Build the routine | Keep progressing and look back at your log |

## Exercises to start with

Pick one exercise for each main movement:

- **Squat:** goblet squat or barbell [squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squat_(exercise))
- **Hinge:** [Romanian deadlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_deadlift) with dumbbells or a barbell
- **Push:** [push-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push-up) or dumbbell bench press
- **Pull:** lat pulldown or dumbbell row
- **Core:** [plank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plank_(exercise))

The [full body workout plan for beginners](/blog/full-body-workout-plan-for-beginners) gives a complete session layout.

## Your first 4 weeks in the gym: key habits

1. **Schedule fixed days**, such as Monday, Wednesday, Friday. [How often should beginners work out](/blog/how-often-should-beginners-work-out) helps you choose.
2. **Start lighter than you think.** [How to choose weight at the gym](/blog/how-to-choose-weight-at-the-gym) explains how to find the right load.
3. **Log every set**, even the light ones. See [how to log sets during your workout](/blog/how-to-log-sets-during-your-workout).
4. **Rest between sets** long enough to keep form clean. See [how long to rest between sets](/blog/how-long-to-rest-between-sets).

## An example

Illustrative goblet squat progress over the month, 3 sets:

| Week | Weight | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 8 kg | 10, 10, 10 |
| 2 | 12 kg | 10, 10, 9 |
| 3 | 12 kg | 12, 11, 10 |
| 4 | 14 kg | 10, 10, 10 |

Weeks 1 and 2 found the right weight. Weeks 3 and 4 started real progression.

## What to expect

The first sessions can feel awkward. New exercises take practice, and it is normal to need a few sessions before a movement feels smooth. Your weights may rise quickly early on as you learn the lifts and find the right loads. That is why it helps to write down every number from day one: the log shows how far you have come by the end of the month.

## After week 4

Keep the same plan and keep adding reps or weight. The [beginner's guide to strength training](/blog/beginners-guide-to-strength-training) and [progressive overload for beginners](/blog/progressive-overload-for-beginners) cover what comes next. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
