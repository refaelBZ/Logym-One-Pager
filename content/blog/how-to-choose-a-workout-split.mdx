To choose a workout split, start with one question: how many days per week can you train consistently? For 2 to 3 days, a full body split usually fits best. For 4 days, an upper/lower split is a common choice. For 5 to 6 days, push/pull/legs is popular. The best split is the one that matches your schedule and that you can repeat for months.

## What a workout split is

A split is how you divide your exercises across the week. Some splits train the whole body each session. Others focus each day on certain muscle groups or movements. The split sets your weekly structure; the exercises, sets, and reps fill it in.

## How to choose a workout split in four steps

1. **Count your realistic training days.** Use the number you can keep on a busy week, not your best week.
2. **Match a split to that number** using the table below.
3. **Check session length.** Fewer days usually means longer sessions.
4. **Commit for at least several weeks** so you can see progress in your log.

## Common splits at a glance

| Days per week | Split | Each session covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 to 3 | [Full body](/blog/full-body-workout-plan-for-beginners) | All major movements |
| 4 | [Upper/lower](/blog/upper-lower-split) | Upper body or lower body |
| 3 or 6 | [Push/pull/legs](/blog/push-pull-legs-program) | Pushing, pulling, or legs |
| 3 | [5x5 program](/blog/5x5-workout-program) | A few big barbell lifts |

For a direct comparison of training frequencies, see [3-day vs 4-day vs 5-day splits](/blog/3-day-vs-4-day-vs-5-day-split).

## What matters more than the split

- **Consistency.** A simple split you follow beats a perfect one you skip.
- **Progressive overload.** Adding reps or weight over time drives progress on any split. The [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) guide explains how.
- **Tracking.** Logging your sets shows whether the split is working.

Beginners can start with full body training. The guide on [how often beginners should work out](/blog/how-often-should-beginners-work-out) covers that choice.

## An example

An illustrative weekly plan for someone who can train Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday:

| Day | Session |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Upper body |
| Wednesday | Lower body |
| Friday | Upper body |
| Saturday | Lower body |

Four days fit an upper/lower split. If one day drops out regularly, switching to three full body sessions keeps every muscle group trained.

## When to change your split

Change when your schedule changes, not every time you feel bored. Most splits need weeks to show results. [How long to follow a workout program](/blog/how-long-to-follow-a-workout-program) and [how to customize a workout plan](/blog/how-to-customize-a-workout-plan) help with that decision. If a lift causes pain, stop and consult a qualified professional.
